Marketing and Content own the what and the why. This case study is about the how.

So I've kept to that. Everything here is about production: how Capitalmind's videos are shot, cut, designed and shipped.

1Finding 1

The same episode got 14× the views when it was filmed and packaged

Five episodes on the channel were uploaded twice, with identical titles. One version is the audio with the podcast artwork as a still image. The other is the filmed episode with a designed thumbnail.

What it means for the studio

Same speakers, same argument, same title. Three pairs run to the same minute; two are slightly different edits. Most of the gap comes from having a picture worth watching and a thumbnail worth clicking. That's what a studio adds, measured on Capitalmind's own channel.

The still-image uploads should stop. They split the audience for an episode that already has a better version.

Six thumbnail styles on one channel

Podcast stills

Six looks make it hard for a viewer to spot a Capitalmind video in a feed before reading the channel name.

Across the 60 uploads I count six visual approaches: the purple mutual fund set, dark photos with big condensed type, cash-pile and crash-chart designs, podcast stills, numbered series art, and older mint templates.

What I'd do

Build one thumbnail system from the two strongest styles already in use. The purple mutual fund set is the most consistent work on the channel. The dark condensed style reads well at phone size. Together they give fixed type, a fixed face position, and one accent colour per business.

Every new upload uses the system, and every thumbnail gets tested with YouTube's Test & Compare, which picks the winner on watch time, not clicks.

3Finding 3

Every recording is published once

38 of the 60 videos run 45 minutes or longer, and long videos do well here. “Make your own pension” runs 1 hour 28 minutes and has 44,000 views.

But each long recording goes out as one video. The only vertical clip on the page, “Saving more is better than high returns”, runs 3 minutes and was uploaded as a horizontal video with blurred sides. It still passed 8,900 views, more than 35 of the other 59 videos.

What I'd do

Plan the vertical cut at the shoot. Frame each speaker so a 9:16 crop works, keep mics and graphics out of the crop line, and mark the three to five strongest moments on the run sheet. One shoot day then delivers the full episode plus a set of Shorts, without a reshoot or an awkward crop.

The spread inside that group is the useful part. “A Dynamic Multi Asset Fund for Uncertain Markets” and “Why Smart Money Is Moving Into Arbitrage Funds” were built as explainers and reached 8,800 and 7,200. “Surge India”, a 2-minute strategy promo, has 439, the lowest on the page.

What I'd do

Make product videos in the same format as everything else: the investor's question first, then how it works, then where the fund fits. Shoot them with the same kit and the same thumbnail system as the rest of the channel.

For the day-to-day product shoots, I'd keep a standing setup (lighting, camera positions, lower thirds) that turns around in hours.

Part 2 · The plan

How I'll make Capitalmind easy to recognise

My plan for video, design and brand consistency at the Capitalmind studio.

The problem

You know a Zerodha post before you read the name. Capitalmind doesn't have that yet.

When you scroll past a Zerodha post, a Groww video or an INDmoney video, you know whose it is straight away. Zerodha's blue, Groww's green, INDmoney's navy. The colour, the fonts and the layout tell you before the logo does.

Capitalmind's research is some of the best in the market. But its videos, thumbnails, posts and newsletters don't look like they come from the same company. So people see the content and still don't connect it back to Capitalmind.

Other BFSI brands
Capitalmind
Why this matters

People remember brands by colour and shape before they remember the name

Jenni Romaniuk at the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute has studied this for years. Her research calls them “distinctive brand assets”: colours, logos and characters that make people think of one brand even without the name. For an asset to work it needs two things:

FameLots of people link it to your brand.
UniquenessIt links only to your brand, not to anyone else.

Capitalmind has neither right now, because the look keeps changing from post to post. Nobody can learn something that keeps changing.

In finance this matters even more. People give their money to names they already know and trust.

B · In the edit

The first 30 seconds decide the video, so every video gets ten ways to start

YouTube Studio has a metric called “Intro” that shows what share of viewers are still watching after the first 30 seconds. YouTube's own advice is to change those first 30 seconds and try different styles.

With three or more camera angles, one recording can open in many ways: a close-up on the strongest line, a wide shot in the middle of an argument, a chart on screen before anyone speaks.

For every video I'll cut around ten openings, test them as Shorts and Reels, and use the one that keeps the most people watching as the opening of the full episode.

Every video follows the same script structure and the same editing rules

Script structure for every series
  1. The hook: the strongest line or question, right at the start
  2. The question the viewer has in their mind
  3. The explanation, with the data on screen
  4. The takeaway, in one sentence
Editing guidelines for the whole studio
  • One colour grade for each set
  • Fixed fonts for titles, lower thirds and on-screen numbers
  • One design for lower thirds and name cards
  • A small approved library of transitions, sound effects and music
  • One caption style, on every video
  • One intro and one end card per series

This also makes the work faster. Editors and the agency don't have to start from zero on every video.

AI can give us the shots an office can't

Some topics need visuals we can't shoot in an office: a concept explained in motion, a cutaway that sets the mood, a scene that shows an idea. AI tools can make these quickly, and they'll look like Capitalmind's own instead of the same stock footage everyone else uses.

The rules I'll follow
  • No realistic-looking fake people, and no made-up market data or charts. Real numbers only come from Capitalmind's research.
  • YouTube requires creators to label realistic AI content that could be “mistaken for a real person, place, scene, or event”. Clearly animated or unrealistic visuals don't need the label. We'll follow this on every upload.
  • Every AI shot is marked in the edit file and goes through the same compliance check as everything else.
  • AI supports the story. The team on camera tells it.

Compliance is part of the template, so it never slows the video down

Capitalmind is SEBI-registered, so the studio has to get this right every time.

Mutual fund videos: SEBI's advertisement code asks for the standard warning in video ads to be shown on screen and spoken in a voice-over, “in a clear and understandable manner”. The code gives 5 seconds as an example of clear. I'll build this into a fixed end card, so no mutual fund video goes out without it.

Every number on screen gets checked before the final export.

A fixed compliance review slot every week, so reviews are planned and not rushed at the last minute.
